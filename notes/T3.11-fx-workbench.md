## Summary

- **Two effects in portable C99** (`fx/`): a tape delay with wow, flutter, crackle and up to two extra
  heads, and a 3-band master compressor with Linkwitz-Riley crossovers and a global sidechain. 32-bit
  float only, no libc, no malloc, no division per sample (only at a crackle event), all state in one
  struct.
  **Both compile with selache's `selcc` for the ADSP-21569 unchanged.** [verified]
- **A renderer** (`fxrender`) that runs them in 32-sample blocks as the DSP does, with knob values
  0..127, timed changes and sweeps. **Test material** is synthesised (`fxgen`), since there are no
  recordings yet.
- **76 tests pass** (`make test`): the crossover sums flat to 0.0003 dB, the static curve matches the
  formula to 0.0001 dB, attack and release match their time constants to the sample, delay times are
  exact to 0.0001 sample, 32-sample blocks give bit-identical output to any other cut of the stream,
  and 420 s of extreme settings give no NaN, Inf or denormal anywhere. [verified]
- **Cost:** with a good compiler or hand assembly, about 7k instructions per block for the delay
  (stock: ~6k) and 12-14k for the compressor (stock: ~19k) [inferred, hand count]. **selcc's code is
  much slower than that**: 59k and 137k per block (static count, same method as T3.4), because it
  inlines nothing and keeps every value in memory. Before the unit, the inner loops need restructuring
  or hand assembly (section 6). [verified for the counts]
- **The flanger** has a slot (`fx/flanger.c`, pass-through) and nothing else, until the yflanger notes.
- **Listen:** `out/T3.11/ab.html` (section 7).
- **2026-10-06:** the page now has the **stock DN2 effects, emulated** (the unit's own DSP code in the
  T3.6 interpreter, not a recording) next to the new ones on the same material, at the closest stock
  knobs (section 10). The new effects' SHARC cost, now measured exactly: **delay 18.4-26.8k, compressor
  47.7-54.7k instructions per block** (was 44.4-55.8k and 102-117k; stock 5.4k and 16.6k), with the

Common rules for both effects [verified by the tests unless marked]:
- **Knob values** are the DN2's 0..127 (fractional allowed, since TIME and FDBK carry a fractional low byte
  in the frame, T3.3); enumerations take their index. Setters only store the value; the mapping to
  physical units runs at the next control update.
- **Control rate:** every 32 samples of the stream (1,500 Hz), whatever the caller's block size. The
  smoothed values ramp linearly per sample inside each control block. That is why any cut of the
  stream gives bit-identical output.
- **Smoothing:** gains and mixes glide with a 20 ms time constant, crossovers 40 ms, the delay time
  with a tape-like glide (120 ms, at most 0.5 samples per sample, so playback speed stays within
  0.5x..1.5x), wow/flutter depth 0.3 s.
- **Denormals:** every recursive state is flushed once per control block (add and subtract 1e-18), the
  tape ring and the outputs per sample, and every glide snaps to its target when it gets within 1e-6.
  Inside a block a fast-decaying filter state can still pass through a denormal on the Mac; the SHARC
  flushes those in hardware [inferred: SHARC floating point has no gradual underflow].
- **State size:** `TapeDelay` 3,146,060 bytes (the stock delay owns 4,194,908 bytes of DDR, T3.4), so it
  fits in the stock delay's place. `MBComp` 532 bytes (stock: 376 bytes in L1); it would go in the free
  L1 block 2 (T3.2).

## 2. Tape delay: design and proposed page

*Superseded on 2026-10-06 by section 12.4 (one head, AGE, resonant filters, WID). Kept as the record.*

Signal flow (per channel): `in + FDBK x playback -> tone (1-pole HPF, 2-pole LPF) -> soft clip -> tape
ring`. The main head reads the ring with 4-point Hermite interpolation at `TIME + wow + flutter`; crackle
is added to what it plays, and that sum is both the wet output and the feedback. Up to two extra heads
read the same ring (linear interpolation, same wow and flutter), panned left and right, into the
wet output only. The output is wet only, as the stock delay's is: the FX bus mixes it with VOL and
sends some of it to the reverb.

**Proposed page (SEND FX 1), 8 encoders + LEVEL, the stock positions kept where the meaning is the
same:**

| enc | stock | new | range and mapping |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | TIME | **TIME** | as stock: `(v+1)/128` of a whole note at the project tempo, so 64 = a half note; 8 s at most (30 BPM). The 128th-note unit is [inferred] from the stock constants (30/300 BPM clamp, 384,000-sample maximum = a whole note at 30 BPM, T3.4) and Elektron's manuals |
| B | X (ping-pong) | **FDBK** | 0..110 %; above 100 % the soft clip holds the level (self-oscillation without blowing up) |
| C | WID | **TONE** | bipolar. 64 = tape band, HPF 50 Hz + LPF 6.5 kHz. Down to 0: LPF falls to 700 Hz (darker repeats). Up to 127: HPF rises to 1.5 kHz and LPF to 16 kHz (thinner, brighter). The filters sit in the loop, so each repeat gets more filtered |
| D | FDBK | **WOW** | 0..2 % pitch deviation, square-law taper (32 = 0.12 %, 64 = 0.5 %). A 0.55 Hz sine plus slow random drift |
| E | HPF | **FLUT** | 0..0.8 %, square-law taper. 6.5 Hz and 11.3 Hz sines plus faster jitter |
| F | LPF | **CRKL** | 0 = off. Up: 1..60 events per second and louder. Each event is an impulse through a random band-pass: 85 % "ticks" (2-7 kHz), 15 % "pops" (300-1000 Hz), random pan. Added to the playback, so pops also echo |
| G | (none) | **TAPS** | 8 head patterns, 16 values each: `1` one head; `PP` ping-pong; `1/2` heads at 1/2 and 1; `3/4`; `TRI` 1/3, 2/3, 1 (Space-Echo-like); `1/2+3/4`; `DOT` 3/8, 3/4, 1; `PP+1/2`. Extra heads play at 0.7 of the main head, one panned left, one right; only the main head feeds back. Switching patterns fades the old heads out and the new ones in |
| H | REV | **REV** | unchanged (delay to reverb send) |
| LEVEL | VOL | **VOL** | unchanged |

- **Left off the page:** DRIV (tape drive into the soft clip, fixed at 32: +3.9 dB; 0..127 = 0..+15.6 dB).
  Gone from stock: WID (the extra heads are panned instead), separate HPF and LPF (TONE replaces both),
  and the ping-pong switch (now two TAPS patterns). [guess] for which ones the owner will miss.
- **Frame words:** the stock delay block is `k` 32..40, nine words, and this page needs nine (seven
  effect knobs, REV, VOL). `k` 40 (Delay FX Routing) is never read by the stock DSP (T3.4), so it is
  free. So the new page fits the stock frame; only the ColdFire side (labels, ranges) changes, through
  the core (T1.9).
- **Alternative, if DRIV or WID should be on the page:** fold WOW and FLUT into one `W&F` knob (wow first,
  flutter joining past the middle) and put DRIV on E. Owner's choice.
- **A DSP-only first version** (no ColdFire change at all, stock labels on screen): TIME and FDBK as
  they are, WID (bipolar) as wow/flutter (left of centre wow, right flutter), HPF as TONE, LPF as CRKL,
  X as ping-pong. The screen would show the wrong names, but it could be heard on the unit before the
  core exists. [guess] whether that is worth doing.
- Constants marked [guess] in the code (LFO rates, noise mix, crackle spectra, head gain 0.7, glide
  times) are first choices, to be tuned by ear.

## 3. 3-band compressor: design and proposed pages

*Superseded on 2026-10-06 by sections 12.2-12.3 (each band the stock design, MUP, one sidechain). Kept as the record.*

Signal flow: Linkwitz-Riley 4th-order split at the lower crossover into low and the rest; the rest
splits at the upper crossover into mid and high; the low band goes through the second-order allpass of
the upper crossover. Then `low + mid + high` is the product of two allpasses: flat magnitude, whatever
the two frequencies (tested to 0.0003 dB). Each LR4 split is three Butterworth state-variable filters
(the first one shared by the LP and HP paths), so the crossovers can sweep without zipper noise.

Each band: peak level `max(|L|,|R|)` in dB (stereo-linked), static curve (threshold, ratio, soft knee),
then attack/release smoothing of the gain reduction in dB, then makeup gain (after Giannoulis, Massberg
and Reiss, JAES 2012). Attack and release are time constants (63 % of a step). A band in KEY mode
listens to the sidechain instead of itself; the sidechain is the full-band input (SCS internal) or the
external key, through the SCF filter. The dry path goes through the same two allpasses, so MIX below
127 does not comb-filter at the crossovers.

**Proposed pages:** three band pages and a global page. The band pages show each band's own edges:
the low page its upper edge, the mid page both, the high page its lower edge. The two crossover values
are shared, so the low page's HI and the mid page's LO are the same number.

| enc | LOW page | MID page | HIGH page | range and mapping |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | THR | THR | THR | -60..0 dB, linear in dB (as the stock THR, T3.4: `60*v-60`) |
| B | RAT | RAT | RAT | 1:1..20:1, exponential: 0 = 1:1, 32 = 2.1, 64 = 4.5, 96 = 9.6, 127 = 20 (the stock RAT has 8 steps, 1.5..20) |
| C | ATK | ATK | ATK | 0.1..100 ms, log (64 = 3.3 ms) |
| D | REL | REL | REL | 10..2000 ms, log (64 = 144 ms) |
| E | GAIN | GAIN | GAIN | makeup, bipolar -18..+17.7 dB, 64 = 0 dB. Negative values make it a band cut as well |
| F | MODE | MODE | MODE | COMP (band detects itself), KEY (band detects the sidechain), BYP (no compression; GAIN still applies) |
| G | HI (= XLO) | LO (= XLO) | LO (= XHI) | 30 Hz..16 kHz, log, about 0.85 semitone per step. Defaults 38 = 196 Hz and 90 = 2.6 kHz. If LO is turned above HI the two are swapped internally (still flat) |
| H | free | HI (= XHI) | free | see open questions |
| LEVEL | VOL | VOL | VOL | pattern volume, as on the stock MIXER pages |

**Global page** (the stock MIXER 1 compressor page; SCS, SCF and MIX keep their places):

| enc | stock | new | range |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | THR | **THR** | offset added to all three thresholds, bipolar -24..+23.6 dB: one knob to push the whole thing harder |
| B | ATK | **KNEE** | soft-knee width 0..24 dB, all bands |
| C | REL | **TIME** | scales every attack and release, bipolar x0.25..x3.9 |
| D | MUP | **OUT** | output gain, bipolar -18..+17.7 dB |
| E | RAT | **LSTN** | listen: ALL, LOW, MID, HIGH (solo one band, crossfaded) |
| F | SCS | **SCS** | sidechain source. Here: internal or external key; on the unit the stock list (tracks, inputs) would choose what the master stage feeds as the key [inferred from T3.4: the master reads SCS at `0x1c1fef` to pick the sidechain input] |
| G | SCF | **SCF** | sidechain filter, bipolar: below 64 a low-pass from 16 kHz down to 100 Hz, 64 off, above 64 a high-pass from 20 Hz up to 5 kHz |
| H | MIX | **MIX** | dry/wet 0..100 % |

- **Frame words:** 3 x 6 band values + 2 crossovers + 8 global = 28 words. The stock compressor block is
  8 words (`k` 60..67). So 20 more words have to travel from the ColdFire to the DSP: an open question
  for T6.2 (unused frame space, or a second frame field the core adds).
- **Meters:** `mbcomp_gr_db()` returns each band's gain reduction, for a GR meter per page if the core
  can draw one.

## 4. How to run

From `fx/` (clang and python3 only; binaries go to `out/T3.11/bin/`):

```sh
make              # fxrender, fxgen, fxtest
make test         # the tests below, a few seconds
make renders      # dry material, all A/B renders, out/T3.11/ab.html (under 10 s)
make listen       # serve out/T3.11 at http://127.0.0.1:8711/ab.html (gapless switching)
make sharc        # selcc build for the ADSP-21569 + instruction count -> out/T3.11/sharc/cost.txt
make bench        # Mac speed

# one render by hand: knob values 0..127, -at for a change, -ramp for a sweep
../out/T3.11/bin/fxrender -fx tapedelay -i ../out/T3.11/dry/pluck.wav -o /tmp/x.wav \
    -p TIME=23 -p FDBK=70 -p WOW=60 -p RES=90 -ramp 2 6 AGE 0 127 -at 6 WID=127 -bpm 120 -tail 4 -vol 64
../out/T3.11/bin/fxrender -fx mbcomp -i in.wav -key kick.wav -o out.wav -p SCS=1 -p M.RAT=0 -p H.RAT=0 -meter
../out/T3.11/bin/fxrender -fx flanger -i pad.wav -o fl.wav -p FDBK=110 -vol 127 -gain -12
../out/T3.11/bin/fxrender -fx mbcomp -list      # parameter names (also tapedelay, flanger)
../out/T3.11/bin/fxrender -bands -i any.wav     # RMS of the three compressor bands (2026-10-06)
```

`fxrender` reads 16/24/32-bit PCM and float WAV at 48 kHz (mono or stereo) and writes 16-bit dithered by
default (`-bits 24|32`). Knob events land between 32-sample blocks, like a frame update. `-mix send`
(default for the delay) outputs `dry + VOL/127 x wet` [guess: the stock VOL curve is not known; T3.4
shows the bus multiplies the return by the VOL float]; `-mix insert` (default for the compressor)
outputs the effect only; `-mix wet` the delay's wet signal alone.

## 6. Cost

**Hand count of floating-point operations per stereo sample** (adds, multiplies, compares, loads of
ring data; from the C source) [inferred]:

| effect, part | ops / sample | notes |
|---|---:|---|
| tape delay: smoothing (glide, ramps) | ~10 | |
| main head: index, 4-point Hermite x 2 channels | ~60 | 8 ring loads (DDR) |
| crackle: counter, 2 band-pass SVFs | ~30 | an event (~900 instructions in selcc) only 1..60 times a second |
| 2 extra heads: index, linear read x 2 channels, pan | ~50 | skipped when TAPS has no extra head |
| record: input/ping-pong mix, HPF, 2-pole LPF, soft clip x 2 channels | ~70 | |
| **tape delay total** | **~170-220** | **x 32 = 5.5k-7k per block**; stock delay ~6.3k (T3.4) |
| compressor: crossover, 14 SVFs (2 channels) | ~170 | 12 ops each |
| dry path, 4 SVFs | ~50 | skipped at MIX 127 |
| sidechain key, 2 SVFs + level | ~30 | only when a band is in KEY mode |
| 3 x (level, log2, curve, smoothing, exp2) | ~140 | log2 and exp2 are short polynomials, no `powf`/`log` calls |
| output: weights, mix, gain | ~25 | |
| **compressor total** | **~340-420** | **x 32 = 11k-13.5k per block**; stock compressor ~18.7k (T3.4), which calls `powf` and `log` per sample |

So on a good compiler or in hand assembly, the tape delay costs about what the stock delay costs, and
the 3-band compressor less than the stock 1-band one. The SHARC's parallel multiply-add and SIMD stereo
mode could do better still. [inferred]

**selcc, measured** (`make sharc`; static count, same method as T3.4: every instruction once, times its
loop trips, plus callees; both arms of every branch, so an upper bound) [verified]:

| | per 32-sample block | per sample | vs stock |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| tape delay, steady (knobs still) | 59,450 | 1,858 | stock delay ~6,300: about 9x |
| tape delay, a knob moving and a crackle event every sample (worst) | 89,719 | 2,804 | |
| compressor, steady | 136,943 | 4,279 | stock compressor ~18,700: about 7x |
| compressor, a knob moving every block | 140,840 | 4,401 | |

Why selcc's code is so large [verified by reading `out/T3.11/sharc/*.s`]:
- **It inlines nothing**, `static inline` included, and optimisation flags change nothing (`-O0`, `-O1`
  and `-Oa` give the same 4,079 lines for `mbcomp.s`). Every SVF step, `fabs`, `max`, `log2` and `exp2`
  is a call with a full frame: `fx_svf_tick` is 74 instructions for 12 floating-point operations.
- **Every value lives in memory**: struct fields are reached by computing the address into `I4` and
  loading, every time, and loop counters stay on the stack. No multifunction (parallel) instructions.
- An experiment, `fx/sharc_svf_block.c`: the same SVF written as a 32-sample loop with its state in local
  variables (the shape the stock code uses) compiles to **42 instructions per sample**, against roughly
  85-90 for a call of `fx_svf_tick`. Hand-written SHARC assembly would need about 5-6 per sample, or
  half that per channel in SIMD mode. [inferred for the hand figure]

What this means for the unit:
- The budget: at 1 GHz (the ADSP-21569's maximum; the unit's clock is unknown, T3.4) a block has
  666,667 cycles, and the whole stock render is about 258k instructions by the same static method
  (T3.4). Swapping in the selcc builds as they are would add about 190k per block (59k + 137k - 6k
  - 19k): too close to the edge, before DDR stalls. [inferred]
- **The route:** restructure the inner loops as per-stage 32-sample loops with locals (roughly halves
  selcc's cost), then hand-write the hot loops (the SVF banks, the Hermite reads) in SHARC assembly, or
  improve selcc's inlining and register allocation upstream. The Mac code stays the reference: the SHARC
  build has to match it sample for sample (or within float tolerance) through the T3.6 interpreter.
- Mac speed (`make bench`, Apple silicon, clang -O2): tape delay 26 ns per stereo sample (800x real
  time), compressor 71 ns (300x). Relative numbers only.

## 7. The A/B page: `out/T3.11/ab.html`

`make listen` in `fx/` serves `out/T3.11` and prints the address (http://127.0.0.1:8711/ab.html). Each
group holds the dry input and its renders; the buttons or keys 1-9 switch versions while it plays, space
plays and pauses, "match loudness" turns the louder versions down to the quietest. Over http every
version of a group is decoded and plays in sync, and switching is a 5 ms crossfade (gapless,
sample-aligned) [verified in the browser pane: decoding, sync, switching]. Opened directly as a file, the
browser blocks that, so the page falls back to one player at a time and seeks on each switch (a short
gap) [not tested: the pane cannot load local files].

Groups and renders (41 files; each has its description and settings on the page):

| group | renders |
|---|---|
| Tape delay on a plucked melody | clean echo; light wow/flutter; worn tape; three heads (TRI); TIME jumps (tape glide); everything |
| Wow and flutter on a held pad | no wow; WOW sweep 0 -> 127; flutter only; dark loop (TONE 20) |
| Crackle and head patterns on single hits | CRKL sweep 0 -> 127; TAPS 1, PP, 1/2, TRI, DOT; pattern changes while playing |
| Feedback and saturation on chord stabs | runaway feedback held by the soft clip; bright thin loop |
| Tape delay on drums | eighth-note tape echo |
| 3-band compressor on a mix | 1:1 (crossover only); gentle glue; heavy; listen low / mid / high; crossover sweep (mid soloed); heavy parallel (MIX 64) |
| One band at a time, on drums | low band squashed; high band tamed; mid band with slow attack |
| Sidechain: a kick ducks the bass | low band in KEY mode; all bands in KEY mode |

Delay renders use the send mix at VOL 64 (`dry + 0.5 x wet`); compressor renders are the effect's output.
Stock recordings dropped into `out/T3.11/stock/` (T3.5) appear as their own group after `make renders`.

## 8. Open questions for the owner

1. **Delay page:** is TIME, FDBK, TONE, WOW, FLUT, CRKL, TAPS, REV right? Would you rather have DRIV (tape
   drive) or WID on the page, with wow and flutter on one knob? Are the 8 head patterns the useful ones?
2. **Delay feel:** the TIME glide (tape-motor pitch bend, up to 1.5x speed) versus a clean jump or a
   crossfade when TIME moves. Crackle in silence: the tape crackles even with no input; should it follow
   the signal instead?
3. **Compressor pages:** the band pages each have their own edge(s), and the low and high pages have one
   free encoder (H). Candidates: per-band RANGE (maximum gain reduction), detector PEAK/RMS, a per-band
   KNEE (it is global now), or lookahead. What would you use?
4. **Ratio:** continuous 1:1..20:1 (exponential), or the stock's 8 steps?

Measured with test signals through `tools/sharcvm/fx.py` (`FxRunner`) before choosing the mapping.

| delay TIME | echo of a 1 kHz burst, TIME 0..31, 120 BPM, after 4 s of settling | (TIME+1) x 750 + 24 samples: the same law as the new delay, 24 samples later |

### 10.4 The T3.7 comparison script: `scripts/t37_compare.py`

For when the owner's recordings exist (`out/T3.11/stock/`, names as in the T3.5 plan). For each take
it renders the same dry file at the same settings in the emulator (`tools/sharcvm/render_fx.py`, new
`render_file()`: raw knobs through `stockmap`, settling pre-roll, send mix, side-chain key) and
reports, per take: the **lag** of the recording behind the render (10 ms then 1 ms envelopes, then
samples, parabolic sub-sample peak) and the correlation there, the **clock drift** (a second
alignment late in the file, ppm), the least-squares **gain** (dB; also relative to the `path_` takes,
which measure the converters and the unit's dry path), the **residual** after lag and gain are
matched (dB below the recording: near -40 dB or lower = nearly the same signal), and the
**spectrum**: third-octave bands 31.5 Hz..16 kHz of recording minus render after the level match
(mean |dev|, worst band). Settings are the T3.5 table's screen values (TIME v+1, bipolar v-64, RAT
as the ratio), overridden by `takes.txt` lines (`take_name KEY VALUE, ...`), so what was really set is
what gets rendered. Output: a table, `out/T3.7/compare.tsv`, `out/T3.7/bands.tsv`; emulated renders
cached in `out/T3.7/emu/`. `--pair a.wav b.wav` compares any two files.

Limits, stated in the script: the emulator renders the effect alone, not the unit's input stage, FX
bus, master overdrive or converters (the `path_` takes measure the converters; `render_full.py` could
do the whole chain later); time-varying effects (chorus, the delay take with a hand-turned TIME,
compared only up to 2.9 s) start at an unknown phase on the unit, so their residual stays high even
if the emulator is right; take `comp_07` assumes unity pan gains in DUAL mode [guess]; "lag relative to
the path takes" only means something if every take was recorded against the same timeline start.
Unipolar knobs are assumed to show v on screen [inferred from T3.3/T3.5].

**Tested without recordings** (`--selftest`, 4 s) [verified]: a render compared with itself (lag 0,
gain 0.00 dB, residual -341 dB); with a copy delayed by 0.3 s + 1,234 samples, 3 dB quieter, noise at
-100 dBFS (lag 15,634.0, gain -3.00 dB, residual -77 dB, spectrum |dev| 0.00 dB); with the same copy
through a one-pole 4 kHz low-pass (spectrum worst band 16 kHz, -8.9 dB; lag 15,634.8). The whole path
(takes directory, `takes.txt` override, the parallel run, the TSVs) was also run on three fake takes
made from emulated renders (lag, gain and residual came out as built); those outputs were deleted.

### 11.2 What changed in the C (the sound did not)

- **No calls in the sample loops.** selcc inlines only a static function called once; every other
  call costs a full frame (`fx_svf_tick` 74 instructions for 12 float operations). The hot loops now
  use statement macros in `fxmath.h` (`FX_SVF`, `FX_LOG2`, `FX_EXP2`, `FX_ABS`, `FX_CLAMP`,
  `FX_UNDENORM`) and `MB_GAIN_STEP`/`TD_GLIDE`; each does the float operations of the old function in
  the same order. `mb_gain()` (used by the tests) is built from the same macro as the loop.
- **State in locals, one loop per block.** `td_run` copies what the loop touches into locals and
  writes it back once. `mb_run` is now four stages, each a loop over the block: the crossover per
  channel (7 SVFs), the dry allpasses (only when MIX < 127), the side-chain level (only with a band in
  KEY), the gain computer per band, the output mix. Work buffers for one block live in the `MBComp`
  struct (1.5 KB; the state is now about 2 KB, still for the free L1 block 2).
- **Per-block decisions taken once:** crackle filters skipped while silent (their state is then
  exactly 0), crackle events worked out before the loop (`td_crackle_plan`), extra heads skipped at
  gain 0, the delay-time glide skipped at its target, and a ring read without wrap checks for all but 3
  read positions.
- **Mac build: `-ffp-contract=off`** (fx/Makefile), as the SHARC has no fused multiply-add. With
  contraction on, clang fused some multiply-adds, so the Mac reference and the SHARC build rounded
  differently. This is the one intended change in the Mac renders: up to -90..-140 dB re peak in most
  renders, but -33 dB in `td_glide` during the TIME glide. That one is the glide's own rounding: the
  delay time is a float near 12,000 samples (one ulp = 0.001 sample) and it is moved by tiny
  increments every sample, so the two roundings drift 0.08-0.3 samples apart (a few microseconds)
  during the glide [verified by tracing `d` in both builds]. Inaudible, but it means only a no-FMA
  build can be compared with the SHARC; a fixed-point or integer+fraction delay time would make the
  glide reproducible across compilers (not changed: sound design).

**Checks [verified]:**
- `make test`: 76 checks, 0 failed.
- All 33 Mac renders, rendered as 32-bit float, **bit-identical** old vs new code, both with the old
  flags (fused) and both with `-ffp-contract=off`.
- SHARC vs Mac (no FMA), all six configurations: the same numbers before and after, to the sample count
  (D2 97.12 % bit-identical, max -166.5 dB; C1 33.02 %, max -122.7 dB; D1 -128.1 dB, D3 -100.4 dB,
  C2 -123.0 dB, C3 -109.7 dB). The remaining differences come from selcc's division and conversions in
  the control-rate coefficient code (not correctly rounded), as T3.6 found. D1/D3/C3 are new
  configurations and show the glide sensitivity above; they were the same with the old code.

### 11.3 Two more selcc bugs, found on the way [verified]

1. **A local initialised with a comparison is re-evaluated at every use**, with the operands' current
   values: `float a = t->a; const int on = a != 0.0f; for (...) { use(on); a = 0.0f; }` gives `on` = 1
   only in the first iteration (repro: 10 lines, `int` and `float` initialisers are fine, only
   comparisons). This broke the first version of the new delay (the crackle flag) at -17 dB. The new
   code sets every such flag with an `if` statement, and the old code's one case (`do_dry` in
   `mb_run`) happened to be harmless. Worth reporting upstream with the union bug of T3.6.
2. Not a bug but a trap: selcc **inlines a static function that is called once** (`td_run` and
   `mb_run` end up inside `*_process`), so `sharc_cost.py`'s static per-function count no longer
   finds them; `make sharc` prints the dynamic counts instead.

### 11.4 What is left for hand assembly

selcc keeps every local in a stack slot across loop iterations, moves values between registers with
`PASS` about once per float operation, never uses the SHARC's parallel multiply/add/load, its
post-modify addressing, or SIMD, and uses compare-and-jump loops when the trip count is not a
constant. In the annotated profile one SVF step costs about 45 instructions for 10 float operations.
That is the gap to the stock code, not the algorithms: the stock compressor calls `powf` and `log` per
sample and still costs 16.6k.

Where the new code spends its time (instructions per block, `--annotate`) and what hand-written
loops would cost [inferred: a cycle count per sample from the operation counts of section 6, with
multifunction instructions and no stalls]:

| part | selcc now | hand assembly | notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| compressor crossover, 14 SVFs x 32 | 20,500 | 2,700 (1,400 in SIMD) | 5-6 cycles per SVF step; L and R in SIMD halve it |
| compressor gain computer, 3 bands x 32 (log2, curve, smoothing, exp2) | 16,000 | 2,500 | 20-25 cycles per band-sample; the polynomials map onto multiply-accumulate |
| compressor output mix | 4,200 | 400 | |
| delay main head (index, 4-point Hermite, 2 channels) | 6,000 | 900 | DAG circular buffers would drop the wrap code |
| delay record path (mix, tone filters, saturation, ring write) | 6,100 | 900 | |
| delay crackle (while ringing), extra heads | 2,300 / 4,000 per head pair | 400 / 600 | |
| control updates (both, per block) | 1,500 | 1,500 | rare work, can stay in C |
| **total** | delay 18-27k, compressor 48-55k | **delay 3-5k, compressor 6-8k** | stock: 5.4k and 16.6k |

So: **with hand assembly for those five loops, both new effects would cost less than the stock ones**;
in C through selcc they cost 3-5x stock. The budget view: the whole stock block is about 320k
instructions with no voices (T3.6 4.3); swapping both new effects in as they are now adds about
44k (18.6k + 47.7k - 5.4k - 16.6k), against 190k with the old code. Whether that fits depends on the
unit's real load with voices playing (T4.1, the handler's own EMUCLK word). The plan stays: the C is
the reference, the assembly loops are checked against it in the interpreter (fxcheck), one loop at a
time.

### 12.2 The stock master compressor, characterised [verified in the emulator, not on the unit]

Method: the disassembly of the four stages (detector `0xb85070`, smoothing `0xb85111`, gain `0xb851e5`,
output `0xb852d2`) gave a per-sample model; `tools/sharcvm/tests/comp_model.py` renders test signals
through the stock code in the T3.6 interpreter and through the model, and reports the largest
difference of the applied gain (every sample where the input is above 0.3 x its peak).

The stock design, stage by stage:
1. **Side-chain filter** (SCF, 12 dB/octave low- or high-pass; 64 = neutral, a gentle high-pass near
   30 Hz) on the side-chain signal (the compressor input, or the SCS source).
2. **Detector: peak, stereo-linked, per sample, no averaging.** `lev = max(|scL|, |scR|)`, floored at
   2^-23, converted to dB with `log10` x 20. Not RMS.
3. **Static curve: hard knee.** Threshold `60 x THR/127 - 60` dB (-60..0). Gain reduction
   `thr + (lev - thr)/r - lev` above the threshold, 0 below. The 8 ratios are a table:
   **1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 16, 20** (RAT is the index 0..7).
4. **Time behaviour: "smooth decoupled" (Giannoulis, Massberg, Reiss 2012), in dB, on the gain
   reduction.** First a release stage with an instant attack, `y1 = min(gc, cr x y1 + (1-cr) x gc)`, then
   a plain one-pole with the attack coefficient, `y2 = ca x y2 + (1-ca) x y1`. So the attack one-pole
   also rounds off the start of every release. Coefficients come from two 128-entry tables
   (`0x2cfe00` ATK, `0x2d0000` REL) indexed by the integer knob. Their time constants follow two power
   laws, fitted here to within float precision of the tables:
   **ATK tau = 0.1 + 99.9 x (v/127)^2 ms** (0.1, 6.44, 25.5, 100 ms at 0, 32, 64, 127) and
   **REL tau = 10 + 1990 x (v/127)^2.5 ms** (10, 73.4, 369, 2009 ms).
5. **Makeup:** `+24.08 x MUP/127` dB (24.08 dB = 20 log10 16: the code subtracts 24.08 x (1 - MUP) in
   dB, then multiplies by 16), added to `y2` before `10^(x/20)`; smoothed over a few blocks.
6. **Wet clip:** the compressed signal is clipped at +-1.0 (`fclip`) before the mix.
7. **MIX: a crossfade with a dB-shaped table** (`0x2d0200`): wet gain `T[MIX]`, dry gain `T[127 - MIX]`,
   where T is 0 dB at 127, -6 dB at 64 (-0.094 dB per step above 64), -24 dB at 32 and -37.5 dB at 8
   (-0.5625 dB per step from 64 down to 8), then about -7.75 dB per step to 1, and 0 at 0. So
   `T[m] + T[127-m]` is 0 dB at 0, 64 and 127 and dips to -2.3 dB around 32 and 96: the "2.3 dB dip"
   of 10.1 is this law, not a fault.

How well the model fits the stock code (max gain error over every sample, dB):

| check | model above | "branching" (one one-pole, attack or release coefficient: the old T3.11 design) |
|---|---:|---:|
| static curve, 3 ratios x 2 thresholds, 1 kHz sine stepping -50..-3 dBFS | 0.04-0.11 | |
| level steps -40 -> -10 -> -40 dBFS, 20:1, six ATK/REL pairs from 0.1 ms/10 ms to 100 ms/2 s | 0.02-0.16 | 1.8-4.3 |
| makeup at MUP 0, 64, 127 | 0.014 | |
| stereo link (R 20 dB below L follows L's gain) | 0.11 | |
| MIX, 11 values: output level vs `T[m] + T[127-m]` | within 0.02 dB at every value | |

The largest errors are single samples at the level changes; settled, the 1 kHz static gain agrees to
0.002 dB, and at 100 Hz the stock gain is 0.08 dB less, which is the neutral side-chain high-pass that
the model leaves out. So the reading of the code above is the stock compressor as the emulator runs
it [verified]; whether the emulator is the unit is T3.7's question.
5. **Added later the same day (owner, verbatim):** "some stereo control would still be great. most
   importantly i think the filters should have resonance, this is what usually makes old delays sound
   cool." So the tape loop gets **resonant filters** (HPF, LPF, RES), a **WID** control (mono -> stereo ->
   ping-pong) comes back, and every effect gets two layout proposals: (a) the best single 8-knob page,
   (b) two pages (16 knobs), since the owner asks whether each send effect could have two pages (another
   agent is researching that). The C implements every parameter of both layouts, so either can be wired.

### 12.3 The 3-band compressor, each band the stock design (`fx/mbcomp.c`)

The owner's notion was already the architecture: two shared Linkwitz-Riley crossovers (XLO, XHI) split
the signal into three bands that do not overlap (each band is -6 dB at its edge and the three sum back
to a flat allpass, tested to 0.0003 dB), each band has its own compressor, and the bands are summed.
What sounded "like an EQ on a band" was the old per-band **GAIN** (bipolar -18..+18 dB: a band level
control) and settings where one band was compressed hard with no makeup, so that band simply got
quieter than the other two. Both are addressed:

- **Each band is now the stock compressor's design** (12.2), with the stock laws: peak detector on
  `max(|L|,|R|)` of the band, hard knee, threshold `60 x v/127 - 60` dB, the stock ratios, "smooth
  decoupled" smoothing in dB (release stage with an instant attack, then the attack one-pole), the
  stock ATK and REL time laws, makeup `24.08 x v/127` dB, and the stock MIX crossfade law.
- **MUP replaces GAIN:** 0..+24 dB, makeup only. No control on a band page can cut a band any more.
- **One sidechain for all bands:** SCS 0 = every band detects itself; SCS 1 = every band detects the
  key input (through SCF). Per-band KEY/BYP modes are gone; a band that should not react gets ratio
  1:1. (The old internal "full-band key" mode is gone too.)
- **Removed:** KNEE (stock is hard), global threshold offset, TIME scale, OUT. 21 parameters instead of 28.
- **LSTN stays, as a setup tool only** (band solo while setting the crossovers); its renders are in a
  group of their own labelled "Setup".

Parameters (C enum order; per band = stock MIXER 1 order):

| | parameter | law |
|---|---|---|
| per band (x3) | THR | -60..0 dB, stock |
| | ATK | 0.1 + 99.9 (v/127)^2 ms, stock (0.1 .. 100 ms) |
| | REL | 10 + 1990 (v/127)^2.5 ms, stock (10 ms .. 2 s) |
| | MUP | 0..+24.08 dB, stock |
| | RAT | index 0..8: **1:1 (band off)**, then the stock 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 16, 20 |
| crossovers | XLO, XHI | 30 Hz..16 kHz log, defaults 196 Hz and 2.6 kHz (unchanged) |
| global | LSTN | setup: ALL, LOW, MID, HIGH |
| | SCS | 0 bands detect themselves, 1 all bands detect the key |
| | SCF | key filter, fitted to the stock corners (12 dB/oct; e.g. 24 = LP 234 Hz, 108 = HP 2.4 kHz; 64 = HP 20 Hz) |
| | MIX | stock crossfade law (64 = both at -6 dB) |

**Deliberate differences from the stock compressor** (each is the owner's call):
1. Ratio has a **1:1 step** (band off) in front of the stock eight, since a 3-band compressor needs a
   way to leave a band alone.
2. ATK and REL are **continuous** (the stock tables have 128 integer steps; the laws are the same).
3. **No wet clip** at +-1.0 (stock clips its compressed signal before the mix): the bus after the
   compressor (master overdrive, T3.4) handles level; the renders are checked for peaks instead.
4. **SCF filters the key only.** With SCS 0 each band detects its own signal; stock filters its
   single detector's input always. Aiming one filter at three detectors would mostly switch bands off.
5. The detector of a band sees the band, not the full signal: that is the point of a 3-band design.

**Layouts.** The compressor cannot fit one page in any case (21 values). Two proposals:

(a) *By band* (3 band pages + 1 global page, the stock MIXER 1 positions kept for A-E):

| enc | LOW | MID | HIGH | GLOBAL |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | THR | THR | THR | free |
| B | ATK | ATK | ATK | free |
| C | REL | REL | REL | free |
| D | MUP | MUP | MUP | free |
| E | RAT | RAT | RAT | LSTN |
| F | free | LO (XLO) | LO (XHI) | SCS |
| G | HI (XLO) | HI (XHI) | free | SCF |
| H | free | free | free | MIX |

Free slots (A-D global, F/H low, G/H high, H mid) are room for later: a global threshold offset, a per-band
range (maximum reduction), detector RMS/peak, a GR meter.

(b) *By function* (3 pages, 24 encoders for 21 values): page 1 THR L/M/H, RAT L/M/H, XLO, XHI; page 2
ATK L/M/H, REL L/M/H, SCS, SCF; page 3 MUP L/M/H, LSTN, MIX, 3 free. Faster for "all three a bit
harder", slower for "set up the low band". [guess] the owner prefers (a): each band behaves like the
stock compressor he knows, on a page of its own.

Frame space: 21 values against the stock compressor's 8 words (`k` 60-67): 13 more, a T6.2 question.

### 12.4 The tape delay: one head, AGE, resonant loop filters, WID (`fx/tapedelay.c`)

Signal flow now (per channel): `WID matrix of (in, FDBK x playback) -> resonant 2-pole HPF -> resonant
2-pole LPF -> drive into the soft saturator -> tape ring -> one head (Hermite read at TIME + wow +
flutter) -> + AGE noise -> wet out and feedback`.

- **One head.** The two extra heads, TAPS and its 8 patterns are gone (and their cost: up to 4k
  instructions per block on the SHARC).
- **How the crackles were made, and how AGE makes them now.** Before: a random event generator fired
  1 to 60 times a second (CRKL), each event an impulse of a fixed size (up to 1.5, plus "pops" 2.5 times
  louder) through a band-pass at a random frequency (ticks 2-7 kHz, pops 300-1000 Hz), added to what the
  head plays, so pops echoed; it ran even in silence, which is why the drum render's tail never decayed.
  Now AGE adds three things that all grow with the knob and **follow the playback level** (a peak
  envelope of what the head plays, 10 ms attack, 1.5 s release), so they live in the echoes and die with
  them: (1) **hiss**, white noise softened by a 6 kHz low-pass, 50 dB under the echoes at AGE 127, 62 dB at
  64, 74 dB at 32; (2) **sparse soft ticks**, 6 x (AGE/127)^2 per second (6/s at 127, 1.5/s at 64), each an
  impulse through a broad band-pass at 1.2-3.5 kHz (no pops), with a random level that is mostly tiny:
  the loudest tick in 6 s was 35.5 dB under the echo peak at AGE 127 and 47 dB at AGE 64 [verified,
  measured on a steady echo]; (3) **top-end loss**: the loop low-pass drops by 0.75 x AGE/127 octave, so
  each repeat is a little duller. At AGE 0 the output does not depend on the noise generators at all
  (tested bit-identical with other seeds). All AGE constants are [guess] for "subtle", to tune by ear.
- **Resonant loop filters** (owner: "the filters should have resonance"). HPF (20 Hz..4 kHz) and LPF
  (100 Hz..18 kHz) are 2-pole state-variable filters in the loop, so every repeat is filtered again.
  RES sets the low-pass Q from 0.71 to 5 and the high-pass Q from 0.71 to 1.4. As RES rises the loop's
  passband is trimmed by (Q/0.707)^-0.4: at full RES the corner rings +17 dB above the passband but
  only +7.2 dB in absolute gain (one pass), so the loop starts to sing at the corner from about 44 %
  FDBK at RES 127 (79 % at RES 64); the saturator after the filters holds a singing loop (peak 0.65 with
  FDBK 110 % and RES 127, tested), and sweeping the cutoff with high RES is click-free (tested). The
  filter corners glide with 20 ms, so a cutoff sweep is smooth. [guess]: the Q range and the trim.
- **WID** (stereo, owner: "some stereo control would still be great"): 0 = mono (the input folded to
  the centre), 64 = the input's own stereo, 127 = ping-pong (the input, summed to mono, enters on the
  left and every repeat crosses sides); continuous in between, click-free when moved (tested: echo
  positions and levels exact for 0, 64 and 127).
- **DRIV** is now a knob (tape drive 0..+15.6 dB into the saturator, level kept; default 32 = +3.9 dB).
- **Two macro knobs for a one-page layout:** TONE (the old bipolar tone, now through the resonant
  filters: 64 = 50 Hz..6.5 kHz, down to 0 the low-pass falls to 300 Hz, up to 127 the high-pass rises to
  2.5 kHz) sets HPF and LPF; W&F sets WOW (0.9 x v) and FLUT (v - 40 above 40). They write the
  underlying knobs, so either layout can be wired to the same C.

**Layouts.** (a) **One page** (8 encoders + LEVEL), the best compromise:

| A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H | LEVEL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TIME | FDBK | TONE (macro: HPF+LPF) | RES | WID | W&F (macro) | AGE | REV | VOL |

Merged: HPF/LPF into TONE, WOW/FLUT into W&F. Left at their defaults: DRIV (+3.9 dB). (Alternative:
DRIV instead of AGE on G, AGE fixed at about 24.)

(b) **Two pages** (16 encoders):

| page | A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 DELAY | TIME | FDBK | LPF | RES | HPF | WID | DRIV | REV |
| 2 TAPE | WOW | FLUT | AGE | free | free | free | free | free |

LEVEL = VOL on both. Five free slots on page 2; candidates (not implemented, each cheap): DUCK (wet
ducked while the input plays), OFS (right channel time offset, a second stereo control), wow and
flutter rates, a TIME glide mode (tape glide or jump), FREEZE (repeat forever, input muted).

**Ping-pong and stereo** came back as WID (above), so the question "whether and how ping-pong could
come back" is answered by the owner's new request; it costs nothing at WID 64 (the matrix is skipped)
and 1.8k instructions per block otherwise (measured, 12.7).

### 12.5 The flanger in the chorus slot (`fx/flanger.c`)

A classic design of our own, shaped by the traits the owner likes in Max for Live's y-flanger (a
feature summary he sent; that device's code was not read or imitated). Per channel: `in -> 1-pole HPF ->
+ soft-limited feedback -> ring -> Hermite read at d(t) -> POL -> wet out`, and `read -> DAMP low-pass ->
FDBK -> cubic soft clip -> back into the ring`.

- **Output and the send slot.** The effect outputs the delayed signal only, like the stock chorus; the
  FX bus adds the dry track. With send and VOL at unity the sum is the textbook flanger
  `x(t) + x(t - d(t))` with full-depth notches; a lower send or VOL gives shallower notches (the send
  level is the "mix").
- **Delay range 0.05 ms to 77 ms**, wider than a classic flanger (0.1-10 ms), so MAN reaches chorus (10-25
  ms), slapback (50-77 ms) and pitched combs. MAN is **one semitone per step** as a comb pitch:
  `d = 0.050221 ms x 2^(MAN/12)`, so MAN 66 = 2.273 ms = A4 (440 Hz), 78 = A3, 90 = A2, 54 = A5.
- **The sweep** moves the delay in octaves (log2): DPTH is the half-range (0..3.32 octaves each way,
  so 100:1 at full depth), MAN the centre; where the range would leave 0.05..77 ms it is cut to fit and
  the LFO still spans all of it. A triangle then sweeps at an even rate in pitch; a sine slows down at
  both ends (the "swell").
- **Smooth delay motion:** per 32-sample block the sweep is evaluated at the start, middle and end and
  the delay runs on the quadratic through those points (forward differences: two additions per sample,
  no exp per sample, no pitch corner every 32 samples). The middle point uses the settings halfway
  through their glide (a first version used the end values there and put a bump into every block while
  MAN, DPTH or OFS moved: found by the click test, fixed).
- **LFO:** six shapes (SHP: sine, triangle, saw up, saw down, square, random step) and **SLEW**, a lag on
  the LFO's output from 1 ms to 0.5 s, so square and step shapes give "zaps" without clicks. Rate free
  (SPD 0.02 Hz..10 Hz, log: 50 s per cycle at 0, 0.46 Hz at 64) or **SYNC** to the project tempo (one
  cycle per 16, 8, 4, 2, 1 bars, 1/2, 1/4., 1/4, 1/8., 1/8, 1/16, 1/32; the DSP gets the BPM like the tape
  delay). The phase is a 32-bit integer: exact rate, no drift. Not done: phase alignment to the bar
  (that needs the sequencer position, not only the tempo).
- **Stereo:** WDTH = the right LFO 0..180 degrees ahead (the sweep rotates across the field); OFS = the
  right delay 0..5 ms longer (square law: 0.32 ms at 32, 1.3 ms at 64), width without chorus smear.
- **Feedback:** FDBK bipolar -99..+99 % (64 = 0), positive = classic bright sweep, negative = hollow,
  nasal (resonances at odd multiples). **DAMP** is a one-pole low-pass in the loop (0 = none, 12 = about
  10 kHz, 48 = 2.6 kHz, 64 = 1.6 kHz, 127 = 300 Hz); the read delay is shortened by that filter's own delay at low
  frequencies, `(1-a)/a` samples, so a ringing loop is in tune. With a short MAN and FDBK near 99 % the
  loop is a **Karplus-Strong string**: a drum hit or a noise burst rings at 1/MAN, DAMP sets how fast the
  highs die. Measured pitch error of the ringing: within 0.2 cents for A2..E5 at DAMP 12, 1.6 cents at
  A4 with DAMP 64 (1.6 kHz) [verified, `make test`]. The fed-back signal is soft-limited (the cubic clip of
  fxmath.h, at most 1.0), so high feedback saturates instead of running away.
- **POL** inverts the wet signal (crossfaded over about 30 ms). Decision on "a separate wet-polarity
  switch": worth having, but only on the 2-page layout. With the bus adding the dry, inverting the wet
  moves the notches (a notch at 0 Hz and at multiples of 1/d instead of between them): together with
  negative FDBK it is the classic "negative flanging". On one page, FDBK's sign covers most of it.
- **HPF** on the flanger's input (the whole wet path, 20 Hz..2 kHz): the lows stay dry and do not turn to
  mud at high feedback.
- **Through-zero flanging: left out.** In a send slot the bus adds the undelayed dry signal; a through-zero
  design needs the reference path delayed too, so it cannot cancel cleanly there (it would need the
  insert position or a send and VOL known to be exactly 1).
- **Memory:** two rings of 4,096 + 3 samples (the 3 repeat the first 3, so a 4-point read never wraps):
  33 KB in all. That is more than the stock chorus's 4,928 bytes of DDR (T3.4, T3.2 2.6), so it needs a
  home of its own: L1 block 2 (128 KB free, T3.2, single-cycle; it would share it with the compressor's
  2 KB and spliced code), free L2 (466 KB), or the 1 MB the new tape delay leaves unused inside the stock
  delay's 4 MB of DDR (3.15 MB used). A build with `-DFL_RING=512` (10 ms longest delay, 4.1 KB) fits the
  stock chorus's own space if needed.
- **Default patch** (slow, deep, resonant): DPTH 96 (0.16..5 ms), SPD 30 (0.087 Hz, 11.5 s per sweep), sine,
  FDBK 107 (+68 %), DAMP 12, MAN 50 (0.9 ms), WDTH 48 (68 degrees), HPF 32 (64 Hz).

**Knob map and layouts.** Stock chorus page (T3.3): A DPTH, B SPD, C HPF, D WDTH, E -, F -, G DEL, H REV,
LEVEL VOL; DEL and REV are the bus's chorus sends and stay.

(a) **One page:** A DPTH, B SPD, C HPF, D WDTH, E FDBK, F MAN, G DEL, H REV, LEVEL VOL. The stock
positions kept where the meaning matches (DPTH, SPD, HPF, WDTH, DEL, REV, VOL; WDTH changes from "width"
to "stereo phase", which is what width means for a flanger), the two free encoders get FDBK and MAN.
Fixed: SHP sine, SLEW minimum, SYNC off, DAMP 12 (10 kHz), OFS 0, POL normal. Alternative for comb and
string sounds: DAMP instead of HPF on C (HPF fixed at about 60 Hz).

(b) **Two pages:**

| page | A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 FLANGER | DPTH | SPD | HPF | WDTH | FDBK | MAN | DEL | REV |
| 2 LFO & LOOP | SHP | SLEW | SYNC | OFS | DAMP | POL | free | free |

Two free slots; candidates (not implemented): an envelope follower that pushes the sweep (a classic
"auto-flange"), LFO phase reset on a trig, a low-pass on the wet output.

the compressor got cheaper (47.7k -> 44.9k: no knee; and each band left at 1:1 saves about 5k, C4), the delay a
little dearer (18.4k -> 19.1k clean: two resonant 2-pole filters per channel instead of a 1-pole and a
2-pole). The flanger costs about what the stock chorus does, in plain selcc C.

**Budget** (from the prior-art survey, notes/prior-art-2026-10.md: the SHARC runs at 1 GHz, 666,667
cycles per block, and the stock firmware measured on a unit sits at 63-65 % DSP load, so about 35 %,
some 233k cycles per block, is free). Swapping all three at the defaults adds (21.5 - 5.4) + (44.9 - 16.6) +
(16.7 - 14.5) = **46.5k instructions per block (7 % of the block)**, 57.5k in the worst configurations
(8.6 %). That fits the free 35 % even in selcc's code, before the hand assembly of section 11.4. Caveat:
instructions are not cycles (DDR stalls, the delay ring is in DDR) [inferred]; the handler's own EMUCLK
word on the unit (T4.1) is the real check.

**Two more selcc bugs, found by fxcheck** [verified, each with a repro and a workaround in the code]:
3. **A static const table indexed by a fresh integer can read a wild address.** In
   `int i = (int)(clampf(k[2], 0.0f, 3.0f) + 0.5f); if (i > 0) return b * (1.0f / 60.0f) / T[i];`
   (8 lines with a 4-entry `T` and a static `clampf`) selcc keeps `i` in R3, then loads the constant
   1/60 into R3 and scales that as the index. The flanger's tempo sync trapped on it (read of
   0xf24e224c). Workaround: read the table in a statement of its own (`beats = T[i]; return b / (60.0f *
   beats);`), checked in the assembly.
4. **In `float a = X, b = a;` the store of `a` is dropped**: `a` is read later from a slot that was never
   written (repro with `snip.py`: native 55, SHARC 50). It froze the flanger's random-step LFO and made
   the delay's `TD_GLIDE` macro (`float e_ = ..., d_ = e_ - target;`) diverge by -0.4 dB in the worst
   configuration; that macro is older than this section, so section 11's checks happened not to hit it [inferred]. Workaround:
   one declaration per variable; a scan of all four sources finds no other instance.
Both are worth reporting upstream with the two of section 11.3 (T3.6 note, item 6).

**Negative shift immediates** (selache's assembler mis-encodes them, and on silicon they park the core,
per the prior-art survey): **selcc emits none** [verified]: it always loads the shift count into a
register (`R1 = -0x11; R3 = LSHIFT R4 BY R1;` for `x >> 17`), and the four generated files contain 0
immediate-count shifts (`make sharc` now counts them and prints the number). libsel's runtime avoids
them on purpose (a comment in `runtime.s` describes the same hang). The effect code was also made
shift-free where it was easy (the log2 exponent, the noise generators: a 32-bit LCG instead of xorshift),
so nothing depends on it.

- **Compressor numbers on the page.** Every compressor version (new and stock) shows its band levels
  against the dry: each file split with the compressor's own crossover (`fxrender -bands`), RMS per
  band. The page explains in one sentence that compressing one band changes its level against the
  other two, so a heavy single-band setting can sound like an EQ, and that MUP gives the level back.
  Examples: glue new -0.1/+0.2/+0.1 dB (low/mid/high) vs stock -0.2/+0.8/+1.8 dB; bass tamed -0.6/+0.4/0.0
  vs **without makeup -7.9/-0.3/0.0** (the "EQ on a band" effect, here as a demonstration); fast highs/slow
  lows -1.6/+0.2/0.0 (low MUP kept down so the kick's transient does not clip); hats tamed 0.0/0.0/-0.1
  vs stock (full band, keyed by the highs) +0.3/-4.9/-2.8: the stock compressor pulls the mids down
  with the hats.
- **Loudness.** New compressor renders get their per-band level back through MUP, set from a measurement
  (each compressed band's average level change); the stock button's MUP is searched to the new render's
  RMS as before (within 0.3 dB, or capped below clipping: punch -1.5 dB, bass -1.3 dB). "Match loudness"
  starts on in the compressor and the flanger groups. Flanger renders use the input 12 dB down (16 dB for
  the plucks) and VOL 127 (dry + wet at unity: full-depth notches); resonant settings add 10 dB or more.
- **Stock chorus pairing:** its LFO, measured in the emulator, is **0.28 + 2.82 x SPD/127 Hz** (0.28 to 3.1
  Hz, linear) and it sweeps 5.4-7.7 ms; flanger renders get the stock SPD with the nearest rate (the
  flanger's slow swells are below the stock minimum, said on the button) and the same DPTH, HPF, WDTH.

### 12.9 Questions for the owner

1. **AGE:** is it subtle enough, or now too subtle? (AGE 127: hiss 50 dB and the loudest tick 35 dB under
   the echoes.) Should hiss also sit under silence (a constant floor), instead of following the echoes?
2. **Resonance:** the low-pass Q goes to 5 and the loop sings from about 44 % FDBK at full RES. More (Q 10,
   singing earlier) or less? Should the high-pass have as much resonance as the low-pass?
3. **Delay pages:** one page (TIME, FDBK, TONE, RES, WID, W&F, AGE, REV) or two (12.4)? On one page,
   AGE or DRIV on G? On two pages, which of DUCK, OFS, wow/flutter rates, glide mode, FREEZE for the free
   slots?
4. **Compressor pages:** by band (3 band pages + global) or by function (12.3)? Is the 1:1 "band off"
   step in RAT right, or should a band have an explicit on/off?
5. **Compressor differences from stock:** keep the stock wet clip at +-1.0? Should SCF also filter each
   band's own detector when SCS is internal?
6. **Flanger default:** is the default patch the "swelly" sound? Faster or slower, more or less feedback?
7. **Flanger pages:** one page (stock positions + FDBK, MAN; DAMP fixed) or two (12.5)? On one page, HPF
   or DAMP on C?
8. **Flanger memory:** the 77 ms range needs 33 KB (more than the stock chorus's 4.9 KB). Keep the range
   (and find a home in L1 block 2, L2, or the delay's spare DDR), or cut it to 10 ms to fit the stock
   chorus's place?
9. **Tempo sync:** the LFO runs at the synced rate but is not aligned to the bar (that needs the
   sequencer position from the frame). Wanted?
