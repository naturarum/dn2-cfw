### 3.2 The knobs, as the glue reads them [verified in sharcvm and in the Mac build of `se_glue.c`]

### 3.4 Two compiler findings

- **A sixth selcc miscompile** [verified]: with `t->last[k] = v` after a call, k a parameter, selcc forms the store's
  address from the caller-saved R3. A first glue had a helper of that shape: every changed knob value was written
  into the tape at `0x801bd5e8` (seen as a sample of 40.0 in sharcvm), which a head would later play as a full-scale
  blast. The glue now uses constant indices only; every store address was checked by hand in the assembly, and
  `verify_se.py` checks the whole tape (bounds and against the Mac tape). A 9-line repro is in the header of
  `se_glue.c`. Worth reporting to selache with the five of T3.11/T5.2. Other selcc code of that shape is not proven
  safe in general [inferred].
- **Inlining and the stack** [verified]: selcc inlines a static void function called once, and inlined `se_run`
  took the deepest stack point from 1,660 to 2,092 bytes, past sharcvm's 2,032-byte stack (a trap). `se_run` is now
  non-static. The Audio Task's stack is 4,000 bytes; a real interrupt saves a 132-word context on the task stack
  (`0xb8afe4`) [verified in the image]; how many interrupt levels can nest is [inferred].

### 4.2 Code (in core's RAM blob, `.run` 1,275 bytes)

- **fxe_fmt_mode** (`0x47e002dc`): MODE's formatter, `f(value << 8, char *out)`, as the stock ones are called
  (`ParameterSet::vslot23`'s tail jump and `0x400c25a0`); returns the length as sprintf does.
- **fxe_graphid** (`0x47e002b0`, `graph.s`) and **fxe_band** (`0x47e0031a`): at `0x4006f710` the draw asks the page
  id; for page `0x11` (the delay) fxe_graphid rewrites the two locals the stock filter picture is drawn from
  (the caller's `sp@(60)` = CNTR, `sp@(64)` = BW) into the band's lower edge and width by the DSP's law
  (`band_table.h`, generated from `SE_LB0`, `SE_LB_RANGE`, `SE_CTR_OCT` in `fx/spaceecho.c`), then returns `0x11`.

## 6. What reaches the DSP, end to end

ColdFire: knob -> live FX word k (kit) -> raw mirror -> smoother -> frame word `0x9fa + 2 (k - 25)` (TIME `0xa08`,

into the struct `0x257934` (X unscaled as 256 x index); the glue reads the struct and, for AGE, the frame copy
[verified at each step: ColdFire side in the emulator, DSP side in sharcvm; the SPI link itself is the stock one and
was not run end to end]. dnfw's frame census (2026-10-13; T6.0c) agrees that the stock DSP never reads `0xa18`; our
own flip re-run confirmed it [verified].

## 7. Limits, and decisions for the owner

1. **Old kits are not "open".** The brief assumed the stock LPF default is 127; it is **96** (row `0x76`), and kits
   0..3 in the emulator's project hold LPF 96 and HPF 32 [verified]. By the DSP law that is BW 96 around CENTER 32
   (about 200 Hz): an 8.1-octave band, edges near 12 Hz and **3.4 kHz**, no resonance: a gentle 2-pole low-pass
   on every pass [inferred from the law, T5.2 section 4; the band picture shows it]. New kits start the same way.
   Options: leave it (it resembles the stock delay's own darkening at LPF 96 [inferred]), make BW >= 96 count as
   open (a DSP-law change, then re-run `verify_se.py` and the brick checker), or a new default (new kits only).
2. **X = 1 kits** (kit 0 in the emulator's project) load as MODE index 1: head 2 only, at twice the delay time.
3. **MODE's box and text differ for pairs**: the box shows the RE-202 mode number (4 for heads 1+2), the text "12".
   Matching them (text 1..7, or a plain knob glyph) is a one-line change.
4. **WID's ALT side is +6 dB** for echoes already on the tape (-18.7 -> -12.7 dB, measured on the Mac build); back to
   the centre -6 dB; RND has no step. It comes from `spaceecho.c`'s ALT matrices, not the glue.
5. **Loudness**: three heads are summed at unity; below the wet ceiling a held sound with three heads is up to about
   +10 dB over one head (T5.2 question 1, still open). A runaway in any MODE now stops at the ceiling 0.7, +2.5 dB
   over the stock delay's runaway (0.526); in the first build it reached 4.24, +18 dB (section 10) [verified].
6. **MODE glides**: the ColdFire smooths the X word like every frame word, so a jump of several MODE steps passes
   through the modes in between for a few milliseconds [inferred from the T3.3 smoother; not seen on the unit].
7. **DSP time**: instruction counts are not cycles; three heads read 24 floats per sample from the tape in DDR and
   those stalls are not modelled. SE01 is +34,792 instructions per block over stock and +14,261 over FTD1 (mean),
   of the 666,667 cycles a 1 GHz block has [inferred].
8. **Stack**: 1,672 bytes of the Audio Task's 4,000 at most in sharcvm; interrupt nesting not measured [inferred].
9. **Overbridge** sees MODE and AGE values outside 0..1 [unit only].
10. **Going back to GT01**: kits saved on SE01 keep MODE up to 6 and AGE up to 127 in ids 9 and 16. GT01's DSP
    treats any X above 0 as ping-pong on and never reads k 40 [inferred from `td_glue.c`]; how GT01's screen shows
    an X above 1 was not tested.
11. **Not tested anywhere but in simulation**: the sound on the unit, real DSP load, a +Drive project save/load (the
    emulator's eMMC is empty, as in T6.5; kit storage was proven through pattern switching and the stock
    serializer/loader), USB MIDI, Overbridge.

## 10. Review fix (2026-10-13, afternoon): a ceiling on the echo's wet output

A review of the first SE01 build (`out/T5.3/review-dsp/`) reported one major DSP defect. It is real, and it is fixed
in the build above. The main OS did not change.

### 10.1 The defect [verified]

The heads are summed at unity (`se_mix_targets`), ALT and RND place a head at up to sqrt 2 on its side
(`se_pan_gains`), and the feedback is 1.1 x^1.5 times the sum of the active heads (`spaceecho_fb`). Nothing bounded
the wet output: the tape saturator holds every tape sample at +-1, so three heads running away gave up to 3 sqrt 2 =
4.24. Measured in sharcvm on the review's input (a 0.9 sine for 1 s at IN, IN_DEL and DEL_VOL 127, TIME 10, then 3 s of
silence; `out/T5.3/fix-dsp/levels-*.json`):

| build, setting | slot input peak | wet peak | wet peak, last second |
|---|---|---|---|
| first SE01, MODE 6, INT 127, ALT 127 (WID 0), BW 127, AGE 0 | 0.127 | **4.2426** | 4.2426 |
| first SE01, the same at RND 127 (WID 127) | 0.127 | **3.8261** | 3.805 |
| stock, X 1, FDBK 127 (198 % loop), WID 0 | 0.127 | 0.5263 | 0.5262 |
| stock, FDBK 0 (one echo) | 0.127 | 0.1199 | 0 |
| FTD1 (on the unit), FDBK 127, X 1 / X 0 | 0.127 | 0.056 / 0.184 | 0.016 / 0.024 (no held runaway) |

So the first build's held runaway sat 18 dB over the stock delay's. The FX return reaches the master at x14.1254
and then about x3.28 up to a flat 0.693 ceiling (T6.4 section 1.1), so at a high VOL both runaways clip the master;
what the defect changed is how far: at any VOL, 18 dB further than stock's [inferred from the two measured levels and
VOL being a plain gain]. The first flash guide's "about 10 dB louder than one head" was true against the echo's own
single head and understated it against stock. `verify_se.py`'s fuzz moved the knobs every 20 blocks, so it never
held a runaway; its "echo peak 0.165" said nothing about this case. All as the review said.

### 10.2 The fix

`fx/spaceecho.c` (`SE_LIM_T`, `SE_LIM_C`, `SE_LIM_U`, `SE_LIM_Q`, in `se_run` after the hiss and crackle): a memoryless
ceiling on the **wet output only**. Per sample and side, x = |out|: unchanged while x <= 0.5; above, u = min(x - 0.5,
0.4) and the output is +-((0.5 + u) - 1.25 u^2), a quadratic knee that reaches 0.7 with zero slope at x = 0.9 and is
flat beyond. The feedback sums, the tape and its saturator are untouched, so the loop runs as before and only what
leaves the slot is bounded. Add, multiply and compare only: no division, no call, no new state.

Why this remedy, of those T5.2 question 1 and the review named:
- **Scaling the heads by 1/sqrt(n) or 1/n alone does not bound it near stock**: tape at +-1 and the sqrt 2 side gain
  leave 2.45 (1/sqrt 3) or 1.41 (1/3) for three heads at ALT 127, and one hard-panned head can already reach 1.41,
  +8.6 dB over stock's runaway [inferred from that arithmetic; the review's Mac runs measured 1.0-1.33 for one head
  at INT 127]. It would also lower every ordinary echo of MODEs 12
  to 123 by 3 to 9.5 dB, which is the owner's open question 1; the ceiling leaves that question open.
- **The ceiling touches nothing short of a runaway on the unit**: a full-scale input reaches the delay slot at 0.127
  (above; T3.8's fuzz: 0.141 for full-scale noise) [verified]. At 0.13 the Mac build's wet stays at or under 0.394 for
  everything that does not run away (the loudest: three heads, INT 56, RND at full width), below the knee
  (`out/T5.3/fix-dsp/mac-after.txt`) [verified]. The synth voices' sends into the slot were not measured; the voice
  rows and the FX return reach the master at the same x14.1254, and the master's 0.693 ceiling comes at about 0.015
  there, so a voice the master passes unclipped should send far less than 0.13 [inferred]. The knee at 0.5 also
  keeps fx/'s transparency test (white noise at -6 dBFS through one clean head, bit for bit) unchanged.
- **0.7 is +2.5 dB over stock's runaway (0.526)**, inside the review's "about 0.5-0.7".
- **Feedback divided by the number of heads: considered, not done.** It would move the runaway onset to about INT 120
  in every MODE, but it does not bound the level (a runaway still saturates the tape and sums its heads), and the
  onset spread (three heads from about 57, two from 75, one from 120) is the owner's open question 4 and follows
  the RE-201, whose intensity feeds back the sum of the selected heads [inferred, T5.2 section 2]. With the ceiling
  a three-head runaway is at most 1.5 dB louder than a one-head one: at a 0.13 input, one head's runaway holds at
  0.588 (MONO) to 0.696 (RND) and three heads' at 0.700 (Mac build, `mac-after.txt`) [verified].
