## 1. Design [verified on the Mac and in sharcvm]

Our own design from the published ideas on spring modelling (waveguide loops with dispersive allpass chains, the
"spectral delay filter"); no code from other projects. A wave on a helical spring travels faster the higher it is, so
a click comes out as a falling chirp (the drip), and every trip along the spring stretches it more.

```
in L+R -> mono -> half-band 48 -> 24 -> half-band 24 -> 12 kHz            (the tank runs at 12 kHz, as a real one
       -> HPF (TPT one-pole) -> DWEL: drive into a cubic saturator, half made up    passes ~100 Hz..5 kHz)
       -> PRE (pre-delay ring, glided) -> 3 springs in parallel, each a loop:
            v = input + g * HP70(TONE-LP(ring at D))  -> 16 allpasses (c + z^-2)/(1 + c z^-2), c = -DRIP x table
            -> cubic soft clip -> ring;   output tap: ring at D/2 (the far end: first arrival after half a trip)
       -> pan by WDTH (spring 1 left, 2 right, 3 centre, equal power) -> half-bands back to 48 kHz -> wet L, R
```

- **The chirp.** Each stage is a first-order allpass with a negative coefficient (its poles near DC delay the lows),
  stretched to z^-2: twice the delay for the same work, and the delay curve mirrored above 3 kHz (a spring's quieter
  rising "high chirps"). Measured on the impulse response: the energy at 150 Hz arrives 1.6 / 7.1 / 11.2 ms after
  2.4 kHz at DRIP 0 / 64 / 127 (1.6 ms of that is the band-pass used to measure), and the short-time spectral
  centroid falls from 2.9 kHz to 200 Hz over the first ~20 ms. A chirp costs phase (a sweep of T seconds over B Hz
  needs about pi T B radians; one stage gives 2 pi), so the stage count sets both the drip and the cost: 16 stages
  give ~7-11 ms per round trip, more on every echo. A real spring has more (tens of ms on the first arrival).
- **Springs.** Round trips 1 : 1.173 : 1.387 (no simple ratios), slightly less dispersion on the longer ones. TENS
  sets them: at rest each is a whole, even number of samples, so both taps read whole samples (no interpolation loss;
  it shortened long decays by 40 % in the first version).
- **Stable at every setting.** Loop gains from DEC and each spring's round trip, always below 1; the allpasses are
  lossless; the cubic clip bounds every ring sample to 1.0. Fuzzed 120 s (every knob jumping, +-4 bursts): peak 1.86,
  no NaN, Inf or denormal; 300 s of silence after it: exactly 0 everywhere (states below 1e-15 are set to 0;
  FX_UNDENORM was not enough here, section 4).
- **Knob moves.** 50 ms glides; TENS and PRE also slew-limited (0.5 and 0.25 sample per sample), so a turn bends the
  tail's pitch instead of clicking. Click ratio on a held sine: 0.75-1.9 for every knob, TENS 3.3 (the bend, by design).
- **Cut-independent.** One block (32 samples, 0.67 ms) of latency: the resamplers need whole blocks. Calls of any
  length give the same output bit for bit (a FIFO for odd calls; whole 32-sample calls go straight through).
- **selcc.** No division (one-pole coefficients through exp2/log2), one allpass kernel for the springs and the
  half-bands (two stages per pass), state in locals, no table with negative numbers (section 4).

## 2. The page (proposal, revised 2026-10-24: MODE on C, H free)

A-F reuse the stock reverb rows (their storage, CCs and frame words 0xa1a-0xa24), so old kits open with their stock
values; G is row 0x83 (k 48, "Reverb FX Routing", proven on reverb G in the emulator, T6.1). No new row is needed: MODE
folds DRIP and TENS in, so **H stays free**. The values old kits bring give the classic tank.

| knob | label | stock row | range | default | old kits bring |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | PRE | PRE (k 41) | 0..250 ms, square law (32 = 16 ms, 64 = 63 ms) | 0 | 0 |
| B | DEC | DEC (k 42) | RT60 0.3..16 s, log (64 = 2.2 s), of the open tank; trims MODE's | 64 | 64 |
| C | **MODE** | FREQ (k 43) | the character: 0 short and bright .. 64 classic .. 127 long and dark (below) | 64 | 64: the classic tank, the init patch bit for bit |
| D | DWEL | GAIN (k 44) | drive -24..+24 dB into the saturator, half made up; trims MODE's | 64 | 64 |
| E | HPF | HPF (k 45) | 40 Hz..1 kHz, log; trims MODE's | 0 | 0 |
| F | TONE | LPF (k 46) | loop low-pass 600 Hz..9.6 kHz, log (127 = open); trims MODE's | 127 | 127 |
| G | WDTH | row 0x83 (k 48) | 0 mono (a real tank) .. 127 springs 1 and 2 hard left/right | 96 | 1: mono |
| H | (free) | | | | |

LEVEL = VOL. Labels are four characters; the stock dial glyph as a placeholder (T5.0 decision).

**The MODE map** (`spring_mode_map`, a macro layer over the internal knobs; DRIP and TENS stay as the workbench's knobs
and drive the springs only until MODE is set, so every earlier render is byte for byte). Five anchors, the sp_range
characters as knob values, linear in between, which is log in time and frequency since DEC, HPF, TONE and TENS have
log laws. DEC, DWEL, HPF and TONE add their own offsets from their defaults (64, 64, 0, 127) to MODE's value, clamped
to 0..127: at their defaults they change nothing, and they still reach the ends of their ranges.

| MODE | character | DEC | DRIP | DWEL | HPF | TONE | TENS | spread |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | short and bright | 24 (0.64 s) | 40 | 64 (0 dB) | 50 (142 Hz) | 127 | 110 (17 ms) | 0.85 |
| 64 | classic tank = the init patch | 64 (2.2 s) | 64 | 64 | 0 | 127 | 64 (27 ms) | 1 |
| 85 | heavy dwell | 72 (2.9 s) | 96 | 127 (+24 dB) | 0 | 127 | 64 | 1 |
| 106 | surf drip | 80 (3.7 s) | 112 | 100 (+13.5 dB) | 24 (74 Hz) | 110 (6.6 kHz) | 48 (31 ms) | 1.1 |
| 127 | long and dark | 112 (10 s) | 72 | 64 | 0 | 24 (1 kHz) | 28 (38 ms) | 1.25 |

Spread scales the springs' ratios (1 : 1 + 0.173 s : 1 + 0.387 s): closer for the small tank, wider for the dark one.
Order: decay, darkness and spread rise all the way and tension falls all the way (checked at 255 points); the drive
peaks at heavy dwell and the drip at surf. The sp_range settings were moved a little for that: heavy dwell DEC 80 -> 72,
surf 72 -> 80, so the decay never dips. MODE moves glide as the knobs do (50 ms), with TENS's slew limit: a fast move through tension bends the tail (in the
loop at most an octave down or 0.6 octave up, at the output tap half that), a slow sweep hardly at all.

- **selcc = Mac bit for bit**: every output sample of all 117 cases (default, 16 extremes, 81 grid, 13 MODE stills
  and trims, 5 moving incl. MODE, the FIFO path) and the whole Spring state at the end. 0 immediate-count shifts.
- MODE costs about +65 instructions per block when still (the spread's glide). While it moves, every block works out
  the coefficients again (+4.4k, as for any knob moving; the map itself about 500 of that). Its code is +1,242 B
  (`spring_mode_map` 512 B, the rest mostly in `sp_targets`).
- SPNG costs 1.5x the stock reverb: +7.5k instructions per block, about 1.1 % of the 666k-cycle block [inferred:
  instructions as cycles, DDR stalls not counted]. Where it goes: the allpass kernel 9.5k (springs 7.5k), input and
  springs' loops 7.4k, resamplers ~3.5k, control 1k. A cheaper variant: **SP_M 12** (12 stages a spring, `-DSP_M=12`):
  **20,099** per block, the drip about 25 % shorter per round trip (not rendered).

## 4. Findings

- **An eighth selcc miscompile** [verified, repro in the session: `static const float T[] = { -1.0f, -0.5f, ... }`]:
  every negative float constant in a static initialiser is emitted wrong (-1.0f as 0xC0800000 = -4.0, -0.5f as -8.0,
  -1.5f as -3.0; -2.0f happens to survive; integers are fine). Our pan table hit it (spring 1's gains wrong). Fix: no
  negative numbers in static float tables (stored +1, offset in code). No other effect has one (checked: fx/, dspsplice
  src). Worth reporting to selache with the other seven.
- **FX_UNDENORM in a loop holds a step** [verified]: it rounds a tiny value to one step of 1e-25 instead of 0, and a
  loop gain above 0.5 (or an allpass with |c| > 0.5) keeps that step for ever (a DC limit cycle at -500 dB). SPNG sets
  states below 1e-15 to 0 instead (the ring writes per sample, the allpass states 64 floats every fourth block).
- **`make stock` re-rendered 27 stale echo stock files** [verified]: they predated SC02's INT changes, and
  `map_spaceecho` used SC01's per-head law. I restored them byte for byte on 2026-10-23; the main session fixed the
  mapping and the `--jobs` default (4) on 2026-10-24 (notes/T3.11, last section).

and a probe of where SPNG's code goes ([[dsp-code-placement-rule]]).

Knob laws at 0/64/127; silence in gives exact silence; WDTH 0 bit-identical L and R, WDTH 127 correlation 0.23; first
arrival where TENS and PRE put it (within 0.2 ms); RT60 at 1 kHz within 8 % of DEC (DEC 0, 40, 64, 100); TONE 0 shortens

## 7. Renders (`make renders`; the stock reverb pairs after `make stock`) [verified: every earlier file byte for byte
both times: 216 on 2026-10-23, 259 on 2026-10-24]

Five groups, 24 SPNG renders and 14 stock-reverb pairs, input -12 dB (about the unit's send level, which DWEL is set
for), VOL 64. What to listen for:

- **sp_range** (pluck): the init patch, short and bright, surf drip, long and dark, heavy dwell, DRIP 0 (what the
  dispersion adds), WDTH 0. Listen for the falling "pew" on each note and the flutter of the echoes behind it.
- **sp_drums**: the drip on snare hits, the splash of heavy dwell, a tight boingy tank.
- **sp_knobs** (single clave hits): one knob sweeping at a time (DRIP, TENS, TONE, DWEL, PRE); TENS and PRE also
  show the pitch bend while they move.
- **sp_chords**: the resonances on held chords; long and dark.
- **sp_tape**: TAPE alone, SPNG alone, TAPE into SPNG (the echoes drip too), side by side, a dub setting, stabs.
- Each SPNG render's stock row is the stock reverb at the same A, B, E, F knobs (FREQ and GAIN neutral): what the
  same kit plays on the stock firmware, not a match of the sound.
- **MODE (2026-10-24)**, four groups, 16 renders, each with its own stock pair (16): **sp_mode**
  (pluck) and **sp_mode_drums**: MODE 0, 24, 48, 64, 80, 104, 127 with the other knobs at their defaults (MODE 64 is
  sp_def byte for byte); **sp_mode_hits** (clave) and **sp_mode_chords**: MODE swept 0 -> 127 over 12 and 16 s. Listen
  for a continuous path with no dull stretch, the in-between stills (24, 48, 80, 104) sounding as good as the anchors,
  and the sweep's tails bending only gently. Their stock rows take MODE's DEC, HPF and TONE.
